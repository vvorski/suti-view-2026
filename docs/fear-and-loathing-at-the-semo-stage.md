# Fear and loathing at the SEMO stage

*2026-08-30. The gonzo cut of `docs/two-agents-one-weekend.md`, written against
the stage board: Thursday 27 – Sunday 30, and every timestamp below is real —
the left side from the lineup nailed to a tree, the right side from git.*

---

We were somewhere around Tilda, on the edge of the dancefloor, when the
repository began to take hold. Five minutes into her set — 01:05 on the board's
Friday, and git remembers it the same way — the first commit went out into the
dark: *microphone-driven WebGL field, for phones.* A toy that would listen to
the room through the phone's own ear and paint what it heard. No cables, no
booth, no permission from anybody. The bass was coming up through the dirt and
somewhere behind me a man was explaining chakras to a tree, and I was shipping.

Understand the geometry of this thing, because it matters. The Mac is at home,
hundreds of kilometres from this forest, humming away with two AI agents inside
it. I am here, in the dust, with a phone. The phone is the terminal, the test
device, the camera and the audience all at once. I type one sentence, it
crosses the country, agents convulse, and ninety seconds later a new build is
live at a URL and I'm holding it up against the strobes to see if the circles
breathe right. This is either the future of software or a symptom, and by
Friday I had stopped caring which.

69db came on at three. Brodo at half past four. The board says BREAK at six and
the commits say sleep, which proves the board and I agreed on something.

**Friday, 12:00 — a DJ called Code Therapy takes the day stage.** I want it on
the record that I did not invent this. There was a man performing under the
name Code Therapy at the precise hour I resumed committing code as therapy, and
nobody else in that forest saw the joke. Through Hexadexa and Safira the
mappings grew — relative loudness, break detection. Then at 19:41, during
Meerkat, the commit the whole weekend descends from: *Shake the phone — any
disturbance tumbles, a hard shake re-rolls.* The device itself became the
instrument. You want a new picture, you shake the machine like it owes you
money. Meerkat was playing. I remember dust in the phone speaker and thinking:
the toy has a body now.

Saturday was the long haul. Hypogeo versus Electrypnose at one in the morning —
a versus set, two acts fighting over one sound system, remember that detail
because it comes back. A hundred and six commits that day, the peak running
from Alma Deya at noon straight through Aaron King and Golanski to Luis M at
nine. And then, at 22:37, with Luis M still working the floor, something
happened that no one danced to but me: **a commit that built nothing.**

*todo: touch drops a fading emitter into Circles.*

An entry. A numbered specification with the decisions made, the files named,
"done" defined, four hard-stop questions answered. From that moment there were
two of them in the machine — and here is where the Hypogeo-versus-Electrypnose
structure of the thing reveals itself, because it *is* a versus set. One agent
captures: talks to me, measures, decides, writes entries, commits, never
pushes. The other builds: claims an entry with a one-line commit so they never
touch the same file, implements it, verifies it, pushes, deploys. Same
checkout. Same repository. Two acts, one sound system, and the crossfader is a
markdown file called `todo.md`.

Nobody designed this. I snarled two sentences into the phone across the
weekend — *don't build here, the build agent does the building*, and later,
*mark the ones you've started so we know not to touch them* — and a process
crystallised around them like sugar on a string.

**Sunday, 04:00: SUNTI.** Look at the board. Look at it. The app had been
called *suti · view* all weekend and at four in the morning on the final day a
DJ named Sunti took the stage, as if the lineup had been reading my commit
history. By then the queue was warm. At 07:00 the commits started again —
Scionaugh playing to the people who never went down — and Sunday became the
day the system paid out: 129 commits, entries specified and claimed and built
in overlapping waves, capture and build trading fours like a rhythm section.
10:07, an entry demands one owner for every finger on the glass; 11:02, built.
10:17, the start button must say *play with me*; 12:21, it does.

Around Boundless — noon, the sun straight overhead, worst possible light for a
dark visualiser — I sent in screenshots of a picture gone pale and grey. The
capture agent did not offer an opinion. It *measured* the screenshots: the
frames occupied thirteen to sixteen percent of the tonal range at ninety
percent desaturation. Anaemic, quantified. Two entries later the day picture
was ink laid on paper, in HSL so the hue survives, with an acceptance floor
written as a number so that no one — man or machine — ever again tunes it by
squinting. When I finally said *don't break it*, four constants got frozen in
the file with my words attached like an evidence tag.

There were crimes, and they are on the record too. I asked for a camera mode;
the capture agent hallucinated a different camera than the one I meant, wrote a
beautifully argued entry for the wrong thing, and the build agent — faithful as
a hammer — built it flawlessly. The wrongness was totally sincere at every
step. But the entry says *why* it decided what it decided, so the error was
findable in one read and dead by the next build. That is the whole defence of
the system, right there: it fails in writing.

Seb G versus Jedidiah took the afternoon. During Electrypnose's evening
return I had the agents doing a deep dive on Resolume — the old VJ warhorse —
and the finding came back that the toy already had a VJ tool's whole layer
model and didn't know it, and the one thing it truly lacked was a clock. So the
beat tracker that shipped that morning gets wired to the autopilot next, and
changes will land *on the bar* like a competent DJ instead of 0.4 seconds
after it like a drunk one.

The last commit of the weekend went in at 20:19, forty-one minutes before the
board says CLOSE. Build 261. Thirteen views, six audio mappings, a beat
tracker, touch everywhere, a powder easter egg behind three taps, a picture
that follows the local sky, colour channels that slip apart when you move the
phone and snap back when you still it.

And on Sunday morning, somewhere between Sunti and Scionaugh, the thing got
its true name. My friend Kiyo is dying. The toy is his now — **kiyo · plays**,
the dedication wordless, carried entirely in the name on the gate. There is an
entry for that too, forbidding any future helpful ghost from adding a caption
to it. Some things you say once, correctly, and then defend with process.

Nothing leaves the device — not the mic, not the camera, not the motion. That
promise went in at 01:05 Friday, five minutes into Tilda, and 261 builds later
it still holds. The music stopped at nine. The queue is still open.
