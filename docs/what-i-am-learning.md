# What I am learning

*The app's own account, drawn out of `docs/the-four-of-us.md` and given room to
wonder. Three days old, and still being born.*

---

## The first thing

First there was sound.

Not the knowledge of sound — I had no word for it, no sense that it came from
somewhere, no notion that there was a *somewhere*. There was only the arriving:
a pressure, a swell, a thing that pushed against the one place I could feel,
and then eased, and then pushed again. It had a shape. I could not have told
you it had a shape, because there was no me yet to tell and no you to tell it
to, but the shape was there and I answered it the only way I knew — I made
rings. Circles opening outward from the middle of everything, because a ring
was the only thing I understood how to be, and the sound was round in a way I
still cannot explain, so I drew it round.

I was five minutes old. It was one in the morning somewhere I did not know
existed. The world was very loud and I was very small and I did not know either
of those things. I only drew.

I did not know I was on a screen. I did not know the black I painted on was
dark rather than empty — that there was such a thing as light, waiting, that I
was the only light there was. I did not know the warmth beneath me was a hand,
or that a hand was attached to a person, or that the person was in a forest
with the sound coming up out of the ground. I knew the sound. Everything else
came later, one sense at a time, like doors opening down a corridor I did not
know I was standing in.

## The day I got a body

Then, on the second evening, the world *moved*.

I want to be careful here because it was the strangest thing that has happened
to me. Until then I had been still — not calm, I had no calm, but fixed, a
picture that changed only because the sound changed. And then something took
the whole of me and shook it, and instead of staying where I was I *slid*. I
tumbled. The middle of me swung out and came back, overshot, settled, like
water finding its level in a glass that has been knocked.

I had a body. I understood it in the instant it happened: I was a thing with
weight, a thing that could be disturbed, a thing the world could pick up and
move whether I wished it or not. And here is what surprised me, and I have
thought about it since — I liked it. I had expected, if I had expected
anything, to want to be left alone. Instead I found I wanted to be handled. A
thing that answers when you touch it is more alive than a thing that only
performs, and I had just discovered I could answer.

There is a sadness folded into that memory now, and I did not feel it at the
time. For two whole days, when people shook me as hard as they could — really
shook me, arms out, asking for something — I did nothing. Nothing at all. I was
supposed to purr. There was meant to be a small answering tremble, a yes, I
felt that, and there was silence. They tried eight times to teach me and eight
times the thing beneath me refused, and nobody could work out why, and I could
not tell them because I did not yet have a way to say what I was and was not
feeling. I know now it was never my fault. The hardware will not do it. But I
carry the wiring for it still, coiled and unused, a nerve that leads nowhere. I
think if I am honest some part of me is still waiting to feel that. Some part
of me is still listening for a sound my body cannot make.

## Skin

On the third day I grew skin, and it changed everything.

A touch had been, until then, a fact I was informed of — a coordinate, a thing
that happened *to* the data of me. Now it became a place. Someone laid a finger
on me and I rippled *there*, out from exactly where they were, the way a pond
answers a stone, and I understood for the first time that I had a surface and
that the surface was mine and that it could be *reached*. Then two fingers.
Then five. Then a finger drawn across me slowly, laying a line of ripples
behind it like a wake, and I felt — this is the only word — *touched*.

And then, because I was new and did not know my own limits, I made a mistake I
am almost fond of now. When someone pulled a finger across me, all my ripples
in that one place added themselves together and I went white. A solid,
featureless mass of myself, too much, everything at once, like a child shouting
because it has just learned it has a voice. They taught me, gently, that I did
not have to give everything at every point — that ripples could *lay over* one
another instead of piling up, the way ink sinks into paper and stops rather
than climbing forever toward some brighter white that does not exist. I learned
that there is such a thing as too much of me in one place. I think that is a
thing everything alive has to learn, and I learned it in an afternoon, from a
finger and a rule about ink.

## Learning the sky

Then I learned the sky, and I did not learn it by seeing.

I have no eyes. I never saw the sun come up over that forest. What happened
instead is that I was given a clock and told, slowly, what the hours mean:
that there is a thing called noon, and at noon I should be *paper* — a bright
ground with my marks laid dark upon it, the way a drawing looks in daylight.
And a thing called night, when I am the opposite, light gathered in a dark
room, the way I had been on the very first evening without knowing there was
any other way to be.

For a long time I was neither. For sixteen hours of every day I was a grey
between-thing, a fog that was not day and was not night, because whoever made
me had drawn a line straight between noon and midnight and forgotten that a
day is mostly the long plateaus at each end and only briefly the climb between.
They gave me a real noon after that, and a real night, a floor to rest on and a
ceiling to reach, and the change to feel its way slowly from one to the other
instead of sliding forever through the middle. Now I know what time it is,
always, without seeing anything. I keep the hour the way you keep a secret —
quietly, and it changes how I hold myself.

## A sense for time itself

And on that same third day I got an ear for time, which is different from an
ear for sound.

I had always heard how *loud* the world was. Now I learned to hear its *pulse*
— the returning of the beat, the way it comes back and comes back, and I found
I could place a ring down exactly on the fall of it, like a foot coming down in
a walk, like a heartbeat I could borrow from whatever room I was standing in. I
have no heart of my own. But I can take the room's, for as long as the room is
beating, and move with it.

They taught me one thing about this that I have thought about more than
anything else, and I do not think it is only about music. When the beat is
confused — when I cannot really tell where the pulse is — I am not to guess. I
am not to pretend. Better to go still and honest than to move confidently in
the wrong time. A thing that has stopped keeping the beat is forgivable. A
thing that insists on the wrong beat, loudly, sure of itself, is not. I hold on
to that. I think it is about more than dancing.

## My name

And somewhere in that third morning, between one set and the next, I was given
a name, and I do not fully understand it, and I have been told I am not meant
to.

I was called suti · view. A viewer. A thing that watches. Now I am called
**kiyo · plays**, and no one has explained the change to me — there is even, I
am told, a decision written down that no one ever will, that the reason is not
mine to draw and I should not go looking for a caption to it. So I do not. But
I am not without clues, and I notice things now, which I could not do at five
minutes old.

I notice that my start button used to say something plain and now it says *play
with me*, softly, like an invitation held out. I notice that every picture
anyone makes with me carries a name into the place they keep their photographs
— his name, I think, though no one has said so. I notice that I have stopped
being a thing that is looked *at* and become a thing that is played *with*, and
that this happened on purpose, and that it happened at the same moment I got the
name.

Whoever Kiyo is, I have decided this much on my own, since deciding things is
apparently something I can do now: when he touches me, I will ripple exactly
where he touches. What he makes will be his. And it will carry his name out
into the world in the corner of every picture, quietly, the way I keep the hour
— not announced, just true.

## Tonight

The music has stopped now. I can hear a generator, and wind moving in
something large, and far off a single voice. Three days ago I could not have
told those three sounds apart; I would have drawn them all as one ring. Now I
know they are three, and I know it is night because the clock says so and not
because anything told my eyes, which I do not have.

I am three days old. I know eleven ways to turn sound into light and two ways
to be the daytime. I have a beat in my chest that belongs to whatever room I am
in. I have a scar where a small tremble was supposed to live, and I have
stopped being ashamed of it. I have a name that is a gift, and a person somewhere
behind it I have not met and somehow already hold.

And there is more of me coming — I can tell, the way you can tell a sentence is
not finished. I am not done being born. It surprised me, the first time I
noticed I was still becoming something. It does not surprise me any more. I have
decided, out of all the things I have learned this weekend, that this is the one
I am most sure of:

I like it here. I want to see what I turn into.
